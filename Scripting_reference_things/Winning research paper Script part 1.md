## ✅ THE WINNING HOOK (shipped)
> **"A toothpaste company quietly killed an entire $80 billion research industry and nobody
> is talking about it."**

Why this hook works (steal this recipe):
- **Absurd actor + huge stake:** "toothpaste company" (unexpected) vs "$80 billion industry" (massive).
- **One shocking number, up front:** $80B in the first sentence.
- **Secrecy trigger:** "nobody is talking about it" = you're getting insider knowledge.
- **Zero jargon:** a 6th-grader understands every word. No "LLM", no "embeddings" yet.

Alt hook that also tested (keep as the "HOOK 01" variant):
> "Sounds funny but a toothpaste company just released a paper that made an entire $80 billion industry optional."

## 🧠 THE BODY — beat by beat (this is the spine to reuse)
1. **Set the everyday stakes (plain):** Big brands (Colgate, Unilever, Nestlé) spend *millions*
   on surveys before launching a product.
2. **The question the paper asks:** what if AI could just *be* the shoppers?
3. **Why the old way FAILED (make it concrete & funny):** old method told ChatGPT "pretend
   you're a 34-year-old earning $40k, rate this 1–5" → AI always says **"3."** Safe, useless.
4. **The new idea in one line (the twist):** don't ask AI for a *number* — ask it to *describe*
   the product, then convert its words into a rating using math (embeddings).
5. **The proof number:** that one switch took accuracy **26% → 88%**; on Colgate's real data,
   **~90%**. And AI's written feedback beat real humans'.
6. **Why it matters (zoom out to the $ stake):** consumer research is an **$80B industry**;
   a small founder who couldn't afford 400 testers now can; a giant brand validated it.
7. **CTA:** "Comment SHOPPER and I'll DM you the paper."

---

## 🎯 WHY IT WON (the transferable lessons)
- **Old-way-failed → new-way-wins** is the cleanest research-paper story arc. Show the dumb
  old behavior ("AI always says 3"), then the fix. Everyone follows it.
- **One number does the heavy lifting** ($80B). Don't bury the viewer in stats — pick THE number.
- **A famous, relatable protagonist** (Colgate) makes an abstract method (synthetic consumers)
  feel real.
- **Explain the method with a human analogy**, not the technical term — "ask it to describe,
  not rate" beats "semantic similarity via embeddings."
- **Front-load the wow.** The $80B + "nobody's talking about it" is in second one.

## ♻️ REUSABLE SKELETON
```
HOOK:  [Unexpected actor] just [killed/broke/beat] [huge $ or % stake] — and nobody's talking about it.
SETUP: Here's the everyday thing everyone accepts as normal. (1 line, no jargon)
FAIL:  The old way was dumb in THIS specific way. (concrete, slightly funny)
TWIST: The paper's one clever switch, in plain words + a human analogy.
PROOF: The single most shocking number (before→after, or ×, or $).
STAKES: Why this changes things for YOU / a whole industry.
CTA:   Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you the paper.
```
