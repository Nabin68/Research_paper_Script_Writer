## Caption

Almost nobody knows his name, but you use his idea every single day.
Walter Pitts.
In 1943, this self-taught logician, with no degree and barely past his teens, co-wrote the paper that gave birth to the neural network. He did it with neuroscientist Warren McCulloch, and they did it before the first real computer even existed.
Their insight was beautifully simple. A brain neuron either fires or it does not, just like a switch flipping between on and off. They proved a single neuron could handle the basic logic operations AND, OR, and NOT, the same logic inside every chip you own. And a network of them, they showed, could solve any problem a computer can.
That paper led straight to von Neumann's first programmable computers and the 1958 perceptron, which modern AI is built on top of.
We treat AI like it was born in 2022. It was actually born eighty years ago, in a paper most engineers have never read.
