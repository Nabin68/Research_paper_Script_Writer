## Caption

In 2015, four researchers published a paper that quietly made modern AI vision possible.
Apple Face ID, Tesla Autopilot, Google Photos. None of it would look the same without one idea.

The paper is Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition.
Back then everyone believed more layers meant a smarter network. But stack too many and the opposite happened. It did not stop improving. It got worse. Each new layer tried to rebuild the whole answer from scratch, so the depth made it easier to break what was already working.
The fix was almost embarrassingly simple. What if a layer could skip itself when it had nothing to add? They called it a skip connection. A shortcut that carries the original answer forward, while the layer only learns what to add on top.
The results were staggering. A network 152 layers deep hit a 3.57 percent error rate on ImageNet. Better than human vision. It won every major benchmark that year.
One small idea, a decade of breakthroughs built on top of it.
Sometimes the simplest ideas are the most powerful.
