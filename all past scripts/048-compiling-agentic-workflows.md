## Caption

Every AI agent you've used, the ones booking flights, filing claims, closing support tickets runs on something called orchestration.
The model does the thinking, but a second piece of software sits right beside it, telling it what to do at every single step.
Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, all of them build this way.

The flaw is that it never learns.
Ask an agent why your Slack stopped sending, and the orchestrator walks it through the fix.
A week later the same bug returns, and it repeats the exact same steps, burning the exact same tokens to land on the exact same answer.
Waste, on a loop, forever.
So a research team tried the opposite.
Throw the orchestrator away, and train a small model on the procedure until it knows the whole thing cold.
Their 8-billion-parameter model went head to head with Claude Sonnet 4.5, a far larger model, and matched it on quality.
On the hardest test, a 55-step insurance claims process, the small one actually won on naturalness and edge cases for a tiny fraction of the cost.
This isn't here to replace Claude or ChatGPT; those handle anything you throw at them.
It's a specialist, built for the one repetitive task a company runs a million times a day.
And for those tasks, most teams are quietly overpaying many times over.
Smaller and cheaper didn't just keep up. It exposed how much we've been wasting.
