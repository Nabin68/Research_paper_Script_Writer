Stanford just made knee replacement surgeries obsolete with a single injection that regrows your cartilage even in 60-year-old knees. For 100 years, doctors told us one thing, cartilage doesn't grow back. It's the cushion that stops your knee bones from crashing into each other. But Dr. Needy Bhutani and her team at Stanford Medicine just broke that rule. They found a protein in your knees called 15-PGDH that slowly eats your cartilage from the inside as you age. In mice with torn knee ligaments, the same injury footballers and cricketers get, which often turns into arthritis later, the drug stopped it completely. But here's the interesting part. The drug doesn't bring in new cells. It just tells the cells already inside your knee to wake from sleep and start producing new cartilage again. They also tested it on actual human knee tissue, and cartilage started regrowing within just a week. Follow for more research breakdowns like this.
