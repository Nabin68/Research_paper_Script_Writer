## 1. 🟢 Co-Failure Ceiling — one guy audited 67 AIs · **Wow 7.5/10**

**What it is:** [arXiv 2606.27288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27288), solo author **Josef Chen**.
For any system whose output must be *one of the member models' answers* — a router, a majority
vote, a cascade, mixture-of-agents — accuracy can never exceed **1 − β**, where β is the rate at
which **every model is wrong on the same query**. He measures β across **67 models from 21
providers** and shows the industry's standard statistical model underprices it by ~2.5×.

**✅ Fact-check — every number Agent 2 used checks out against the abstract:**

- Open-ended maths: observed **β = 0.052** vs **0.023** predicted under the 67-model Gaussian
  copula → **~2.5× underpriced**, 90% CI **1.7–3.4×**.
- Graded code execution: **β = 0.079**.
- GPQA-Diamond free-response: **β = 0.127** — i.e. **12.7% of hard science questions stump every
  single one of the 67 models simultaneously.**
- VentureBeat's added detail, not in the abstract: naive majority voting across **unequal** models
  had **negative mean gain** — the diverse-but-weaker members outvote the strong one. This is
  Agent 2's "the dumb ones gang up" line, and it is **real and sourced**, not a paraphrase.

**🔑 One correction to the framing — "one guy, no big lab" needs care.** Josef Chen is
**co-founder & CEO of KAIKAKU**, a UK AI/robotics startup, and he has form for solo releases
(his EPICURE food model got its own Digg pickup in May 2026). He is a **founder shipping solo**,
not an unaffiliated hobbyist and not an academic. The §4A underdog framing still works —
*one person grading OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and 18 others* is literally true — but don't say
"some random guy" or "a student." If anyone checks, he has a company. Say **"one founder,
working alone."**

**Wow factors (in order):**
