## 1. One guy audited 67 AIs and found the thing everyone building with AI gets wrong

**Post:** Solo-author study showing that stacking multiple AI models has a hard mathematical ceiling — and that the industry is underpricing joint failure by 2.5× · [When Does Combining Language Models Help? A Co-Failure Ceiling on Routing, Voting, and Mixture-of-Agents Across 67 Frontier Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27288) · [VentureBeat writeup](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/enterprises-using-multiple-ai-models-are-underestimating-failure-rates-by-2-25x)

**Josef Chen** — a single named author, no big lab — tested **67 frontier models across 21 providers** and proved a ceiling nobody selling "multi-model orchestration" wants to talk about. If your system's answer has to be one of the models' answers, your accuracy can never beat `1 − β`, where β is the rate at which *every single model is wrong on the same question*. And β is much worse than the industry's standard metric predicts: observed **0.052 vs 0.023** predicted on open-ended maths — **2.5× underpriced** (90% CI: 1.7–3.4×). It gets worse on harder work: **β = 0.079** on graded code, **β = 0.127** on GPQA-Diamond.

The line that makes the reel: when you vote across diverse-but-**unequal** models, the weak ones **gang up and outvote the smart one**. More models does not mean smarter. It can mean dumber.

- **Why it'll work:** Scores **~75/100**. A=20 (67 models, 21 providers, 2.5×), B=0, **C=15** — and this is the sneaky-good part: the "named underdog vs the giants" archetype (§4A, your *most reliable* hook) is sitting right there, because it's **one person grading OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and 18 other providers at once**. D=12 (enterprises are burning money on routing infra this kills), E=12 (anyone who's ever asked two chatbots and trusted the one that agreed), F=6→high once you use the committee metaphor, **G=10** (clean reversal: more models = worse). Archetype §4E, comps **041 SkillOpt (22.5k)** and **045 Loop Engineering (14.4k)**. Mainstream press is already carrying it, which those two never had.
- **Wow-number to lead with:** **67 models. 21 companies. All of them wrong on the same question 2.5× more often than the industry thinks.** (Backup, more visceral: on the hard science benchmark, **12.7%** of questions stump *every* model simultaneously.)

## 3. The AI that writes its own notes after every mistake — and got 17 points better

**Post:** Self-evolving agentic RL from a 5-university Chinese collaboration; the model extracts its own lessons and trains itself on them · [SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.14777) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)

**Tsinghua, Zhejiang, CUHK, NTU and Tongji** built a loop where the same model plays the task, then reads back its own completed attempts, writes down what it learned **in plain English**, and uses those self-written notes as training signal. No human labels, no external memory at deployment — the skills are training-time scaffolding that gets thrown away.

Numbers are clean: ALFWorld **75.0% → 91.8%** over standard GRPO (a **3B** model), **100%** on the Pick, Look and Clean task families, **86.2% vs 70.9%** on tasks it had never seen. WebShop success **63.3% → 78.9%**. And it hits GRPO's full-data score using **60% of the training data**.
